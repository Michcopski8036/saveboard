# Kids Drawing YouTube Channels — What Their Own Sites Say

I went looking for a drawing channel for a seven-year-old the way most people do: opened a “best drawing channels for kids” roundup and started clicking. The third click landed on a channel’s own website, on the page where it describes its four YouTube channels. Under every single one of them was **Lorem ipsum** — the placeholder Latin a designer leaves behind. The footer said © 2024.

That is not a reason to skip the channel. The videos are free, there are thousands of them, and children plainly enjoy them. But it told me something the roundup hadn’t: nobody writing these lists is opening the sites.

So we opened them. On 5 September 2026 we read the free/paid boundary, the stated ages, and the maker’s own name off each channel’s own website. One thing is worth saying flatly before the list, because a lot of guides get it backwards: **every channel here is free to watch on YouTube.** No account, no subscription. What differs is the gap between what a roundup *describes* and what the YouTube link *opens* — because most of these makers sell something alongside the channel, and roundups tend to describe the product while linking the channel.

“No ads” is where that gap is widest. YouTube shows ads to anyone without Premium. When one of these sites says “ad-free”, it is almost always describing its own paid app or streaming site — not its channel. Art for Kids Hub is refreshingly blunt about this, as you’ll see.

## How we checked, and what we could not check

- **Only official sites.** Every claim below was read off the maker’s own page, not off another roundup.
- **Channel URLs only where the official site links them.** Then we opened each URL and confirmed the channel name in the page’s own metadata. This matters more than it sounds: search for “art for kids hub” and you also get a separate channel called **Art Hub for Kids**, with a different ID.
- **No subscriber counts, video counts or last-upload dates.** YouTube’s channel pages are drawn by JavaScript and its `feeds/videos.xml` endpoint returns 404 to our tools, so any such number here would be copied from someone else’s undated list. We left the column out rather than guess. It is the same discipline we used on [free AI chatbots](/guides/free-ai-chatbots) — read the boundary off the vendor’s own page, and write “not stated” when it isn’t there.

## The list

1. **[Art for Kids Hub](https://youtube.com/artforkidshub)** (free on YouTube, with ads · membership from $5.99 per month) — Made by the Jensen family, and the site names all six of them: “Rob, Teryn, Jack, Hadley, Austin & Olivia”. Their homepage is the most honest page I read all day. It offers “Three ways to create with us” and labels the YouTube option, in its own words, **“Free with ads”**. The paid tier, ArtForKidsHub.tv, is “an ad-free, distraction-free place to create” — $5.99 a month or $64.99 a year after a 7-day trial, with offline downloads and playlists “organized by theme, season, and age”. So when a roundup calls this an ad-free channel, it has quietly copied the .tv page. The site claims “3,000+ step-by-step drawing lessons” and says they cover “every age and skill level”, which is not an age range. No country appears anywhere on the page. Footer: © 2026.
2. **[Cartooning Club How to Draw](https://www.youtube.com/@cartooningclub)** (free on YouTube · Skool community $5 per month) — This is the site with the Lorem ipsum. Its own “Drawing Channels” page lists four channels — Cartooning Club, Cartooning Club Junior, Cartooning Club XL and Cartooning Club Z, plus an older `youtube.com/user/cartooning4kids` address headed “Cartooning Club for Kids” — and the description under each one is still placeholder Latin. The footer reads © 2024. None of that touches the videos, which are free and plentiful: the hub claims “3000+ Drawing Classes” running “From beginner to advanced level”, heavy on Pokémon, Brawl Stars, Fortnite, Star Wars and superheroes. The paid layer is a Skool community at $5 a month, showing 238 members when we looked. The site says it is “Led by me, MRTY, a former professional artist in the film and video game industry”; the community page names him Marty B and credits “senior Art Director roles at Microsoft Games and Activision Blizzard”. No ages, no country.
3. **[Draw So Cute](https://www.youtube.com/drawsocute)** (free on YouTube · merchandise and a donation ask) — Run by Wennie, who signs the site’s posts. It bills itself as “Cute Drawing Videos, Coloring Pages and Crafts for Kids” and pairs the videos with free printable colouring pages and browser games — useful when a child wants the drawing to become something. There is no membership and no app. The money side is a merchandise page and a line on the site: “Any donation will be greatly appreciated and will help to support my production of videos and all activities for this site.” No stated ages, no stated country, footer © 2026. One small thing: the site writes its own channel address as `http://`, so if you are copying it by hand, type `https`.
4. **[Draw With Rob](https://youtube.com/c/robbiddulph72)** (free on YouTube · activity books sold separately) — Rob Biddulph, a children’s author-illustrator, started #DrawWithRob in the 2020 lockdowns: “I decided to post a draw-along video every Tuesday and Thursday that parents could watch with their kids.” On 21 May 2020 the series took a Guinness World Record for the largest online art lesson, “when 45,611 households drew a whale with me”. He is unusually candid about what happened next — the slot moved to “Saturdays at 10am BST/GMT” in September 2020, and “in August 2021 as life returned to (something like) normal, the videos began to be released slightly more sporadically. But I’m still going!” The videos are free and he says so: “Feel free to watch and share them with anybody and everybody.” The paid part is the activity book series. No age range is stated; a “My First Draw With Rob” series exists for the youngest. The page never names a country, though the press contact is a harpercollins.co.uk address and the video times are in BST/GMT.

## Checked and left out

- **“Art Hub for Kids” is not “Art for Kids Hub”.** Two different channels, two different IDs, near-identical names. We link only the address that artforkidshub.com links itself. If a list gives you a bare channel name, you are one click from the wrong one.
- **Mo Willems’ LUNCH DOODLES.** `kennedy-center.org` returned HTTP 403 and a “Just a moment… Enable JavaScript and cookies to continue” interstitial to every request we made. We could not read the page, so we make no claim about the series in either direction — it simply isn’t verified here.
- **drawwithjazza.com has moved.** It now 301-redirects to `www.jazzastudios.com`. Roundups still print the old address.
- **studiojjk.com** (Jarrett J. Krosoczka). The entire visible text the site served us was “National Book Award finalist Jarrett J. Krosoczka”; the rest is built by JavaScript. No channel URL we could verify from the site, so no entry.
- **drawingnow.com and easydrawingguides.com** are drawing sites rather than channels, and neither links a YouTube channel from its homepage.

## If you only want one

- **A parent and child drawing side by side** — Art for Kids Hub. The format is literally a parent and a child at the same table.
- **A child who wants to draw one specific character** — Cartooning Club. The catalogue is organised around games and films.
- **Short, quick, cute, finished in ten minutes** — Draw So Cute, with the free colouring pages as a follow-on.
- **One good picture in one sitting, and you don’t mind an irregular schedule** — Draw With Rob.

## FAQ

### Are these drawing channels free?

Yes. All four are free to watch on YouTube with no account and no subscription. What each maker sells — a streaming site, a paid community, books, merchandise — sits outside YouTube. Free is not the same as ad-free, though: YouTube shows ads to viewers without Premium.

### Which of these channels is ad-free?

None of them, as channels. The “ad-free” wording that turns up in roundups comes from the makers’ paid products. Art for Kids Hub is the clearest example: its own site labels the YouTube option “Free with ads” and reserves “ad-free” for ArtForKidsHub.tv at $5.99 a month.

### What ages are these drawing channels for?

None of the four sites states an age range. Art for Kids Hub says its lessons cover “every age and skill level”; Cartooning Club says “beginner to advanced level”; Draw So Cute and Draw With Rob say nothing numeric at all. Age labels you see beside these names in other lists are the writer’s estimate.

### Why doesn’t this guide list subscriber counts or last upload dates?

Because we could not read them. YouTube’s channel pages render in the browser and its `feeds/videos.xml` endpoint returns 404 to our tools, so any figure would have been copied from another undated list. We would rather have a shorter table that is true.

### How do I make sure I’m opening the right channel?

Use the address the maker’s own site publishes, not a channel name from a roundup. Near-identical names are common — “Art Hub for Kids” and “Art for Kids Hub” are separate channels — and a handle that looks right can belong to someone else entirely.
