# Kids General Knowledge YouTube, Rechecked

Homeschool Pop sits on nearly every list of general-knowledge channels for children, and nearly every one of those lists prints homeschoolpop.com as its site. On 5 September 2026 that address is not a site. The bare domain answers with a 302 redirect to patreon.com/homeschoolpop, and www.homeschoolpop.com serves a Namecheap parking page — “Want a domain name like this? Discover domains on auction now.” The registration itself is intact: created 4 April 2015, updated 4 March 2025, paid through 4 April 2027. Nothing lapsed. There is simply no website there any more, and no round-up we read had noticed.

So this guide is not another set of recommendations. It is a recheck: take the channels that keep circulating on those lists and open each one’s official site to see whether what is being recommended still holds.

## The rule, and the gap we are honest about

**YouTube blocks the fetcher used here.** Channel pages come back as an empty JavaScript shell, and `youtube.com/feeds/videos.xml` returns HTTP 404 for both the `channel_id` and the retired `user` parameter. Consequently this guide carries **no subscriber counts, no video counts and no last-upload dates**. We could not see them, so we did not write them.

**Every channel URL below comes from an official site.** Guessed handles are a real hazard: `youtube.com/@LittleFox` answers as a Chinese-language channel called 妖狐大小姐, not the reading service people mean by that name. Where no official site publishes a channel, the channel is not in this guide.

## Each one, in detail

1. **[TED-Ed](https://www.youtube.com/tededucation)** (animated lessons · no age stated) — The URL is the one in ed.ted.com’s own footer. TED’s organisation page states “TED is a non-profit that believes powerful ideas, powerfully presented, move us”, the copyright line reads “© TED Conferences, LLC”, and the careers section describes TED as “headquartered in New York and Vancouver”. The site addresses students, educators and parents and never names an age band, which matters more here than elsewhere: the catalogue runs from primary-school riddles to philosophy and epidemiology, and the same channel that suits an eleven-year-old will lose a five-year-old completely. Its own impact page claims “1M+ Educators using TED-Ed in classrooms”, which tells you the intended room is a classroom, not a pushchair.
2. **[Khan Academy Kids](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC2ri4rEb8abnNwXvTjg5ARw)** (states “Ages 2-8” · genuinely free) — The clean case, and worth having in the list precisely as a control. The page describes it as a “Free Learning App for Kids Ages 2-8”, says “100% free - forever”, and adds “No ads, no subscriptions - just high-quality learning for every child”. It is the only entry across all three of our guides that states an age in plain numbers *and* commits to being free with no tier above it. Two notes: the page is JavaScript-rendered, so a plain fetch of it returns an empty shell and you have to open it in a real browser; and the free-and-ad-free promise describes **the app**. On YouTube, ads run for everyone without Premium, including here.
3. **[Cosmic Kids Yoga](https://www.youtube.com/c/CosmicKidsYoga)** (movement and mindfulness · not a knowledge channel) — Included honestly: this is yoga and mindfulness told as stories, not general knowledge, and lists that file it under “educational” are stretching the word. The URL and channel ID are the ones cosmickids.com publishes about itself. The site describes the project as “Making healthy screentime since 2011” and the About page names Jaime Amor as its founder; it states no country and no age range, and its footer still reads “© Cosmic Kids 2025”. It is also the most useful example in this guide of the next section, so it gets one there too.

## What “no ads” actually means here

This is where round-ups go wrong, and being sloppy about it produces a false sentence, so here is the careful version.

**YouTube is free.** Every channel above can be watched by anyone, in full, without paying. There is no hidden paywall anywhere in this guide.

The real gap is that the sentences these lists quote as channel features are usually lifted from a **different product on the same company’s website**. Cosmic Kids demonstrates it better than any argument could. Its own site pop-up advertises the app like this: “500+ episodes, including exclusives! No suggested content or algorithms. No ads, ever. Just 100% yoga, mindfulness and FUN!” Read that as a description of the YouTube channel and every clause is wrong — the exclusives are by definition not on YouTube, “no suggested content or algorithms” is a contrast *with* YouTube, and “no ads, ever” is what you are buying. The pricing on the same site: $14.99 a month or $99.00 a year, labelled “BEST VALUE – Save $80.88!”, with a 14-day free trial and a button reading “Watch Ad-Free”. The company is not hiding any of this. Lists just move its sentences next to a YouTube link and change what they mean.

Khan Academy Kids is the same structure with a friendlier ending: “100% free - forever” and “no ads, no subscriptions” are true, and they describe the app. On YouTube you still get YouTube’s ads, because that is YouTube’s business, not Khan Academy’s.

## What we left out, and why

**Homeschool Pop.** With the website gone, there is no official source that publishes a channel URL, states who makes the videos, or gives an age range. We are not going to guess a handle for a children’s channel. The Patreon page the domain forwards to is a membership page rather than an official site, and it does not resolve any of those questions. If the site returns, this entry can be rechecked; until then a link from us would be a link we cannot stand behind.

## Keeping the check, not just the URL

Three sentences are worth more than the three links above: *this one states ages 2 to 8*, *this one is yoga rather than knowledge*, *this one has no website any more*. Paste bare links into a group chat and those sentences are gone by evening.

## FAQ

### Is TED-Ed suitable for a five-year-old?

TED-Ed does not say, which is the honest answer. The site speaks to students, educators and parents without naming an age, and the catalogue spans riddles through to topics well past primary school. Age labels attached to TED-Ed in other guides are the writer’s judgement, not TED’s.

### Why is Cosmic Kids in a general-knowledge guide?

Because it appears on those lists, and we would rather say plainly what it is than quietly drop it. It is movement and mindfulness delivered as stories. It is also the clearest illustration on this page of a company’s app copy being reprinted as if it described its YouTube channel.

### Did Homeschool Pop shut down?

We do not know, and we are not going to say. What is verifiable is only about the **website**: the domain is still registered through April 2027, the bare address forwards to a Patreon page, and www serves a parking page. Whether the YouTube channel is still posting is a separate question we could not check, because YouTube blocks the tooling used for this guide.

### How current is this?

Every site was opened on 5 September 2026 and every quotation is from that day. Domains get forwarded, apps get pulled, and copyright footers go stale — three of those happened inside this one guide — so if you are reading this much later, redo the checks rather than trusting ours.
