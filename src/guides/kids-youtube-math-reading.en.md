# Kids Maths and Reading YouTube, Rechecked

Type alphablocks.tv into a browser today and you do not arrive at Alphablocks. You get a 301 redirect to www.blocksuniverse.tv. So do learningblocks.tv and numberblocks.tv — all three, checked on 5 September 2026, land on the same new domain. The brand is alive and well; it simply moved house. Round-up posts recommending these shows still print the old addresses, and the redirect is doing the work that the writing should have done.

That is what this guide is. Not another set of recommendations — the internet has plenty — but a recheck of the maths and reading channels that keep circulating, done by opening each one’s official site rather than repeating what the last list said.

## The rule we used, and the one thing we could not see

**YouTube blocks the fetcher used here.** Channel pages return a JavaScript shell with no readable body, and `youtube.com/feeds/videos.xml` answers HTTP 404 on both the `channel_id` and the retired `user` parameter. So this guide contains **no subscriber counts, no video counts and no last-upload dates**. We could not see them and we will not estimate them.

**Every YouTube URL below is one an official site publishes.** Not a guessed handle. This matters more than it sounds: `youtube.com/@LittleFox` is not the well-known reading service of that name — it answers as a Chinese-language channel called 妖狐大小姐. Where an official site publishes no channel URL, this guide links the official site instead and says so plainly.

## Each one, in detail

1. **[Numberblocks and Alphablocks](https://www.blocksuniverse.tv/)** (official site · no channel URL published) — The About page reads: “Blocks Universe Ltd (formerly Alphablocks Ltd) is the multi-award-winning home of the global preschool hits: Alphablocks, Numberblocks, Colourblocks and Wonderblocks.” The page title calls them CBeebies shows. Two things a parent should know. First, we searched the home page and the About page and found **no YouTube link at all** — so we do not publish a channel URL for the biggest name in this guide, because no official source gave us one. Second, the site itself is where the useful structure lives: every episode is filed under Level 1 to Level 5, which is the closest thing to an age ladder this brand publishes anywhere, and it does not exist on YouTube. The footer still reads “© 2023 Alphablocks Ltd.” and the site checks your country and shows UK visitors different content from everyone else.
2. **[Numberock](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCt9SZgFExNwWTH5T_JnyF-A)** (maths songs · channel URL from the site’s own metadata) — First, the spelling: the brand is Numberock, one word, though lists usually write it as two. The channel URL above is the one numberock.com publishes about itself, alongside its Facebook, Instagram and Pinterest. The legal name in the terms of service is Numberock Inc., effective 1 September 2025; the site names no country, and the only geographic hint anywhere is a +1 phone number in the terms. It says the videos are “Music Videos Animated by Three Time Emmy Nominated Studio” without naming the studio, and describes its subject as elementary maths and primary numeracy without ever committing to a grade range.
3. **[Storyline Online](https://www.youtube.com/StorylineOnline)** (picture books read by actors · SAG-AFTRA Foundation) — Actors reading picture books aloud, from a foundation: the footer reads “© 2026 SAG-AFTRA Foundation. All rights reserved.” Books are credited by reader — Meryl Streep, James Earl Jones, Oprah Winfrey — and there are Spanish-language titles in the same catalogue. Worth catching before you follow a round-up’s advice: the site’s header still says “Download our free App!”, while further down the same page it says “Our app for iPhone, iPad, and iPod touch is currently not available. As we work to bring it back, for now you can add our website to your home screen.” Both sentences are on the site right now. The lists quote the first one. No age range is stated anywhere on the home page, so any “ages 3–8” you have seen attached to it came from a writer, not the foundation.

## What is on YouTube, and what is somewhere else

Here is the distinction the round-ups blur, stated carefully because the careless version of it is simply untrue.

**Everything above is free to watch on YouTube.** No account, no payment, no trial. Nothing in this guide is a hidden paywall.

What differs is **scope**. A list will describe a *product* — “levelled episodes”, “worksheets and auto-graded assessments”, “printable activity packs” — and then link a *YouTube channel*, where those things are not. Numberock is the clean example: the videos are on YouTube, and the printable games, worksheets, Google Classroom resources, auto-graded assessments, posters, anchor charts and Number Notes are what a numberock.com subscription buys. As listed on its sign-up page on 5 September 2026, that is 30 days free then $4.95 a month, or $29.88 up front for 180 days free and $2.49 a month after, or $149.95 once for lifetime access. None of that makes the channel less free. It just means the sentence describing the curriculum is not a sentence about the channel.

The same move happens with **“ad-free”**. Numberock’s home page says “The world’s best math videos, completely ad-free”, and its terms describe the service as “ad-free math music videos and related instructional materials”. That is the subscription player on its own site. On YouTube, ads run for everyone who is not paying for Premium. A list that copies “ad-free” next to a YouTube link has quietly swapped one product for the other.

And Blocks Universe is the third variant: the recommendation is real, but the levelling that makes the show teachable is on blocksuniverse.tv, not in a YouTube autoplay queue.

## Saving the check, not just the link

What is worth keeping from this page is not three URLs. It is three sentences: *the official site moved and no channel is published*, *the worksheets are a subscription and the videos are not*, *the app the site advertises is currently unavailable*. Send a bare link to a group chat and only the URL survives.

## FAQ

### Why is there no Numberblocks YouTube link?

Because no official page published one. We checked blocksuniverse.tv’s home page and its About page and found no YouTube link anywhere on either. The rule for this guide is that a channel URL has to come from the owner’s own site, so rather than guess a handle we linked the official site, which is where the Level 1 to Level 5 structure lives anyway.

### Is Numberock the same thing as Number Rock?

It is the same product; the brand writes itself as one word, Numberock, and the company name in its terms of service is Numberock Inc. Lists commonly print “Number Rock”, which is why searching that phrase can lead you somewhere else before it leads you here.

### Are these channels good for a four-year-old?

Only one of the three publishes anything close to an answer, and it is not an age: Blocks Universe sorts episodes into Level 1 to Level 5 on its website. Numberock and Storyline Online state no age range at all. Age labels you see in other lists are usually the writer’s estimate presented without being marked as one.

### How current is this?

Every site was opened on 5 September 2026 and the quotes come from that day. Redirects, parked domains and quietly retired apps are the normal failure mode here, so if you are reading this a year from now, the checks are worth repeating rather than trusting.
