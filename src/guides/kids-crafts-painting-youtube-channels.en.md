# Kids’ Craft and Painting YouTube Channels — Sites Checked

The first name in most “kids craft channels” roundups is The Artful Parent. On 5 September 2026 I clicked it and landed on a publication about growing old.

`artfulparent.com` now 301-redirects — bare domain and `www` alike — to `theartfulage.com`, which describes itself as “A weekly letter on aging well, family across generations, and the creative life after the kids leave home” and signs off as “A Brown Brothers Media publication.” Old deep links don’t survive the move either: `artfulparent.com/art-activities-for-kids` forwards to the new domain and returns 404 there.

Nobody updated the roundups. So we went and opened the sites.

Every channel below is free to watch on YouTube — that was never the question. The questions were whether the thing a roundup *describes* is the thing the link *opens*, whether the maker is selling something alongside the channel, and whether the site behind the recommendation is still standing.

## How we checked, and what we could not check

- **Only official sites**, read on 5 September 2026. Nothing here is copied from another list.
- **Channel URLs only where the official site links them**, then opened and matched against the channel’s own name. A site that recommends a channel but never links it gives you nothing to verify.
- **No subscriber counts, video counts or last-upload dates.** YouTube’s channel pages are drawn by JavaScript and `feeds/videos.xml` returns 404 to our tools. Rather than borrow numbers from undated lists, we left them out — the same rule we used when checking [free tiers on basketball sub apps](/guides/basketball-sub-apps).

## The list

1. **[Red Ted Art](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCjjRFKvjpU1L1eDmfBWcqig)** (free on YouTube · books sold separately) — Maggy Woodley, whose channel title spells it out: “Red Ted Art (Maggy Woodley)”. The site introduces her without ceremony — “Hi, I am Maggy! This is my little sidekick Ted.” — and states the mission in capitals: “We aim to **MAKE CRAFTS EASY**.” It claims “over 2500 easy, do-able and fun crafts”. It is the only site in this guide that bands its own material by age, and it does it right in the navigation: “Preschool (0-5yrs)”, “Kids (5yrs+)”, “Tween & Teens”. That is a menu rather than an age rating, but it is more than anyone else offers. No country is stated on the homepage. Footer: © 2026.
2. **[Deep Space Sparkle](https://www.youtube.com/user/DeepSpaceSparkle)** (free on YouTube · memberships with no published prices) — Patty Palmer, in Santa Barbara, California; the site calls itself “a woman-owned and run art education business”. This is the painting entry — real lessons with real materials, not five-minute crafts — and it is also the clearest case in this guide of a roundup describing a product while linking a channel. The business sells three things: **The Sparklers’ Club**, a K-7 art programme for teachers; **Primerry**, online art classes for children; and **Primerry PRO**, coaching for people running paid art classes. **None of the three shows a price anywhere on the site.** The free side is the YouTube channel, the blog, and a handful of downloads such as a supply list. Worth knowing before you arrive: the primary customer here is an art teacher, not a parent. Footer: “Deep Space Sparkle, 2024”.
3. **[Babble Dabble Do](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCxcWYE_GFpLKyRFi_dzlkhQ)** (free on YouTube · a Teachers Pay Teachers shop) — Ana Dziengel, in the Los Angeles area, trained in architecture and industrial design at Cal Poly SLO and Art Center College of Design. It shows: the projects live where art meets science and engineering rather than in seasonal crafts, which makes this the one to open when a child is bored of paper plates. Categories run from “Preschool Art Projects” upward, with no age range stated. The shop link leaves the site for Teachers Pay Teachers, so no prices appear on the page. Footer: © 2026.
4. **[The Best Ideas for Kids](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCuQZN7STmlGaDHaWHng-6AA)** (free on YouTube · two books) — Kimberly McLeod. High volume, low materials — the kind of craft you can start with what is already in the drawer, which is the reason this one gets shared in parent group chats. The site says it serves “kids of all ages” and states no range. Two books are sold via Amazon, with no prices on the site. It also claims “a community of over 5 million followers”; that is the site’s own cross-platform figure, not a channel subscriber count, and we did not verify it. No country stated. Footer: © 2026.
5. **[Messy Little Monster](https://www.youtube.com/c/messylittlemonster)** (free on YouTube · free printables, one book) — “I’m Louise, a creative mum of three and early years teacher.” That is the whole biography on the site, and it is still more than several larger sites give you. The material is the messy end — process art, paint recipes, handprint keepsakes, sensory play — aimed at toddlers and preschoolers. The printables are free. No numeric age range, no country stated, and a copyright line carrying **no year at all**, which makes the site impossible to date from the footer.

## Checked and left out

- **The Artful Parent** — the redirect described above. We could not verify a channel URL from a site that no longer exists at that address, so it is not in the list. If you have it bookmarked for kids’ art, the bookmark now points at a newsletter about ageing.
- **Hello Wonderful** — `hellowonderful.co` returned HTTP 200, but the homepage HTML we received carried Swedish pharmacy text — “apotek i Sverige”, “receptfritt”, “Viagra” — mixed in among the craft content, and contained **no YouTube channel link at all**. We are not in a position to say what happened to the site. We are in a position to say we could not verify a channel from it, so it is out.
- **Mister Maker** — `mistermaker.com` timed out on one attempt and reset the connection on another. No readable page, no entry.
- **Crayola, KiwiCo, The Craft Train, Easy Peasy and Fun, Arty Crafty Kids** — all live and all fine; none links a YouTube channel from its homepage, so we had no official address to trust.
- **TheDadLab** — does link a channel from its own site, but it is science experiments rather than craft and painting. Right idea, wrong guide.

## Picking one

- **A rainy Saturday with paper, scissors and glue** — Red Ted Art, and use the age bands in its menu.
- **A child who wants to paint properly, with a teacher’s structure** — Deep Space Sparkle, expecting a teacher’s vocabulary.
- **Craft that is really science in disguise** — Babble Dabble Do.
- **Nothing in the house but a cardboard box** — The Best Ideas for Kids.
- **A toddler, a tray and a tolerance for mess** — Messy Little Monster.

## FAQ

### Are these craft channels free?

Yes. All five are free to watch on YouTube with no account and no subscription. Books, memberships and shop items sit outside YouTube. Free is not the same as ad-free: YouTube shows ads to anyone without Premium, and none of these five publishes an ad-free version of its channel.

### Which one is best for preschoolers?

Red Ted Art is the only one that bands its own material by age and has a “Preschool (0-5yrs)” section. Messy Little Monster is written by an early years teacher and aims at the same end. Everything else on this list states no ages at all, so treat any age label you see elsewhere as someone’s estimate.

### Does Deep Space Sparkle cost money?

The YouTube channel does not. The business behind it sells three memberships — The Sparklers’ Club, Primerry and Primerry PRO — and, unusually, publishes no price for any of them on its site. If a roundup describes “a full K-7 art curriculum”, that is the membership it is describing, not the channel.

### What happened to The Artful Parent?

Its domain now redirects. `artfulparent.com` and `www.artfulparent.com` both 301 to `theartfulage.com`, a publication about ageing and family, and old article paths 404 at the new domain. That is what we observed on 5 September 2026; we make no claim about why.

### Why are there no subscriber counts in this guide?

Because we could not read them. YouTube channel pages render in the browser and `feeds/videos.xml` returns 404 to our tools, so any number would have come from another list rather than from YouTube. A shorter table that is true beats a fuller one that isn’t.
