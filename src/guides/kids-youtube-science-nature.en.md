# Science YouTube Channels for Kids

Once a child starts asking “but why?”, it helps to have somewhere to send the question. Here are three channels that answer them well, with the age each one suits.

## The three

1. **[SciShow Kids](https://www.youtube.com/user/scishowkids)** (no stated age · free) — Jessi and Squeaks, a robot rat, answer the questions children actually ask — why the sky is blue, where puddles go. They run experiments too.

   It is made by Complexly, the non-profit behind Crash Course. They do not state an age for it.

2. **[Crash Course Kids](https://www.youtube.com/crashcoursekids)** (**3rd–5th grade** · free) — **One of the few channels whose makers state an age outright.** It covers science and humanities, built to sit alongside a classroom or homeschool.

   The flip side: if your child is four, this one is still ahead of them. Same makers as SciShow Kids.

3. **[National Geographic](https://www.youtube.com/@NatGeo)** (not a children’s channel · free) — Wildlife, space and places around the world, filmed beautifully enough that children watch with the sound off.

   One thing to know: **this is the general audience channel, not a children’s one.** Worth picking the video yourself rather than letting it run.

## FAQ

### Are these channels free?

**Every channel here is free to watch on YouTube.** It is YouTube, so ads run. A few of them also sell an app or a membership separately, which is a different thing from the channel — where that applies, it is noted with the channel.

### What ages are they for?

It is noted with each channel. **Fewer channels state an age than you would expect** — where the makers do not say, this guide says so. Watching a few minutes together is still the most accurate way to decide.
