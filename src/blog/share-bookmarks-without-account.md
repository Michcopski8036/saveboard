# How to Share a Bookmark Folder With Someone Who Doesn't Have the App

You've collected a dozen good links — a trip itinerary, a season's worth of training schedules, a reading list for the group — and now you want to hand the whole folder to someone else. Not one link at a time. Not "let me forward these one by one." The whole thing, in one go.

The obvious way — texting every link separately — falls apart past three or four. And a lot of bookmark apps quietly assume the person on the other end already has an account, which turns a 10-second favour into "hey, can you download this app and sign up first?"

Here's what actually works, and what each option gets you.

## The low-tech options

**Copy and paste into a message or email.** Works for a handful of links, gets unreadable past ten, and it's a snapshot — if you add more links later, you're sending it again.

**Export your browser bookmarks to an HTML file.** Every major browser can export a bookmark folder as a file the other person imports into their own browser. It's free and doesn't need any app, but it's one-directional and static: the moment you export, that file stops updating. Add a link the next day and it's not in the file you already sent.

**Browser extensions that share a bookmarks bar folder.** A few exist for Chrome specifically, syncing a shared folder between browsers with no account. They're tied to one browser and don't help if the other person is on their phone, which is where most people actually see links they want saved.

None of these are built for "here's a living board, come back whenever."

## What the bookmark apps actually do

**Raindrop.io** added a public-page feature: turn a collection into a read-only web page with its own link that opens in any browser — the viewer doesn't need a Raindrop account to look at it. That's a genuinely useful feature and worth knowing about if you're already a Raindrop user with a collection to hand off.

**mymind** doesn't share whole collections at all. It can generate a link for a single saved item that stays live for 24 hours before it goes private again — fine for showing someone one thing, not for handing over a folder.

**SaveBoard** works the way the trip-itinerary example above wants: share a board and it opens as a normal web page, no account and no app install needed to view it. If the person wants to keep a copy of the links for themselves, that's when they're asked to make a free account — viewing never requires one.

The honest comparison: SaveBoard and Raindrop both solve "share without an account," Raindrop with a strong desktop-first tool for big libraries, SaveBoard built around saving from your phone's share sheet and sharing back out the same way. mymind, for now, only solves it one item at a time.

## How sharing a board works in SaveBoard

1. Open the board you want to share.
2. Tap **Share** and copy the link.
3. Send it however you'd send anything else — text, email, a group chat.
4. The recipient opens it in their browser. No app, no account, no login screen.
5. If they want their own copy of the links (not just to view them), they tap **Save this board** — that's the one point where an account is needed, and it's free to create.

Update the board later — add a link, remove one — and the same link everyone already has reflects the change. Nobody re-sends anything.

## FAQ

### Can I share a bookmark folder with someone who doesn't have an account?

Yes, with the right tool. Browser bookmark exports and Raindrop.io's public-page feature both let a recipient view a shared collection without signing up. SaveBoard works the same way — anyone with the board's link can open and view it in a browser with no account. An account is only needed if the viewer wants to save their own copy of the links.

### Do they need to download an app to view a shared board?

No. A SaveBoard board link opens as a regular web page in any browser, on desktop or mobile. Having the app makes saving and organising easier going forward, but it isn't required just to look at what was shared.

### Does Raindrop.io let you share a collection without an account?

Yes — Raindrop.io has a public-page feature that turns a collection into a read-only page with its own link, and the viewer doesn't need a Raindrop account to open it.

### Can I share a whole collection in mymind?

Not currently. mymind can generate a temporary link for a single saved item, active for 24 hours, but it doesn't support sharing an entire collection the way Raindrop.io or SaveBoard do.

### Is there a limit to how many links I can share in one board?

Sharing itself isn't separately limited — a shared board shows everything in it. What limits the board's size is your own plan: SaveBoard's free tier covers up to 30 saved links across up to 5 boards with no card required. Pro (A$5.49/month, or A$34.99/year) and Team (A$9.49/month per seat) raise those limits for people saving more.
