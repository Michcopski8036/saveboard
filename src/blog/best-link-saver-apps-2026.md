# 5 Best Link Saver Apps in 2026 (Compared for Everyday Users)

We all save links. Recipes we want to try. Articles we plan to read. Registration forms we need to fill in. Videos we want to watch later.

The problem? Links saved in browser bookmarks, notes apps, or group chats are nearly impossible to find again.

A dedicated link saving app solves this. But which one is right for you? Here's an honest comparison of the five best options in 2026.

---

## 1. SaveBoard — Best for Mobile & Group Chats

**Price:** Free / $3.49 per month Pro  
**Platform:** Android, iOS  
**Best for:** Busy parents, community organisers, anyone saving links from group chats

SaveBoard is the newest entry in this space and the only one built specifically for the mobile-first, group-chat-heavy way most people actually use links today.

When you save a link, SaveBoard automatically pulls the title, description, and preview image to create a beautiful visual card. Links are organised into collections and searchable instantly.

**Strengths:**
- Native mobile app — works seamlessly from the share sheet in WhatsApp, iMessage, and any other app
- Visual card interface — you can see what a link is at a glance, not just a raw URL
- Built for everyday users — no technical knowledge needed
- Collections system is intuitive and fast to set up

**Weaknesses:**
- Newer service — smaller feature set than established tools
- No browser extension yet (coming in 2027)
- Free tier limited to 30 saved links

**Bottom line:** If you primarily save links on your phone and want the cleanest, most visual experience, SaveBoard is the best option in 2026.

---

## 2. Pocket — Best for Read-Later Articles

**Price:** Free / $4.99 per month  
**Platform:** Web, iOS, Android  
**Best for:** People who want to read articles later

Pocket has been around since 2007 and is the original "read later" app. It strips articles down to clean text and lets you save them for offline reading.

**Strengths:**
- Excellent article reading experience
- Good browser extension
- Large, established user base

**Weaknesses:**
- Designed for articles — not great for forms, PDFs, videos, or general links
- Mobile experience is secondary to the web version
- No visual card interface — links appear as a text list

**Bottom line:** Great if you read a lot of articles. Not the right tool for saving links from group chats or managing a variety of link types.

---

## 3. Raindrop.io — Best for Power Users

**Price:** Free / $3.49 per month  
**Platform:** Web-first, limited mobile  
**Best for:** Developers, designers, researchers who manage hundreds of bookmarks

Raindrop.io is a powerful bookmarking tool with nested collections, full-text search, and browser extensions across all major browsers.

**Strengths:**
- Very powerful organisation features
- Good browser extensions
- Partial visual card support

**Weaknesses:**
- Web-first — mobile app feels like an afterthought
- Steep learning curve for casual users
- Not designed for saving links from mobile apps or group chats

**Bottom line:** Excellent for power users who live on desktop. Overkill for everyday mobile users.

---

## 4. Notion Web Clipper — Best if You Already Use Notion

**Price:** Free (requires Notion account)  
**Platform:** Web, iOS, Android  
**Best for:** Notion users who want to keep everything in one place

Notion's web clipper saves pages directly into your Notion workspace. If you're already a Notion power user, this is a natural fit.

**Strengths:**
- Integrates with your existing Notion setup
- Free with any Notion account

**Weaknesses:**
- Requires knowing how to use Notion (complex for newcomers)
- No visual card interface
- Mobile experience is clunky
- Complete overkill if you just want to save links

**Bottom line:** Only worth it if you're already deep in the Notion ecosystem.

---

## 5. Browser Bookmarks — Free But Limited

**Price:** Free  
**Platform:** Desktop only  
**Best for:** Saving website addresses you visit regularly

Good old browser bookmarks are universal and free. But they haven't evolved much in 20 years.

**Strengths:**
- Free and built-in
- Works on desktop

**Weaknesses:**
- No mobile share sheet integration
- No visual previews — just a list of titles and URLs
- Folders become unmanageable quickly
- Not accessible from other devices without syncing

**Bottom line:** Fine for a handful of frequently visited sites. Not useful for saving links from group chats, emails, or mobile apps.
