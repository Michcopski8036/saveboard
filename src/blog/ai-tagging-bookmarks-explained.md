# Do Bookmark Managers Really Tag Your Links With AI? (2026)

"AI tagging" shows up in the marketing copy of most bookmark managers now. It's a good feature to want — nobody enjoys manually filing every link into a folder. But "AI" covers two very different things depending on the app, and it's worth knowing which one you're getting before you pick a tool (or pay for one).

---

## What does "AI tagging" actually mean in a bookmark manager?

There are two common approaches:

1. **Instant automatic categorisation.** The app looks at the link's domain, title, and description the moment you save it, and applies tags like "video," "article," "code," or "recipe" — no waiting, no extra step. This is fast and usually cheap enough to include for free.
2. **A conversational AI assistant.** You can ask the app questions in natural language — "what did I save about flights to Tokyo?" — and it searches and summarises your saved content using a language model. This is more powerful for open-ended recall, but it's heavier to run, so it's more often gated behind a paid plan.

Neither is "fake AI" — they just solve different problems. Knowing which one a tool offers (and whether it's free) matters if that's the feature you're evaluating apps on.

## How SaveBoard's AI tags work

Every link you save to SaveBoard is automatically tagged the instant you save it — no setup, no waiting for processing. SaveBoard reads the link's domain and title/description text and applies relevant tags (for example, a GitHub link gets tagged "code," a YouTube link gets tagged "video," an article with "how to" in the title gets tagged "tutorial"). These AI tags show up on every card and are filterable from the sidebar.

This is the first kind of AI tagging above — instant categorisation, not a chatbot. **It's included free on every plan**, including SaveBoard's free tier — it isn't a Pro upsell.
