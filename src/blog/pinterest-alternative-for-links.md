# The Best Pinterest Alternative for Saving Links (Not Just Photos)

Pinterest is built for images — recipes, outfits, home decor, mood boards. But a lot of people also use it for something it was never designed for: saving links. A secret board becomes the place for a flight itinerary, a registration form, a scheduling doc, an article with no real photo — anything that needs a home and doesn't fit browser bookmarks.

It mostly works. It's also the wrong tool, and it shows in small, annoying ways.

---

## Why Pinterest is awkward for saving links

- **Every Pin needs an image.** Pinterest's save flow is built around picking an image from the page (or uploading one). A page without a decent photo — a Google Form, a plain-text article, a booking confirmation — doesn't turn into a good Pin, if it turns into one at all.
- **Secret boards still require an account to view.** Making a board "secret" keeps it off your public profile, but anyone you invite as a collaborator has to have a Pinterest account to accept the invite and see it. Sharing a private board with a group means asking everyone in it to sign up for Pinterest first.
- **It's a discovery platform first.** Pinterest's whole design — the home feed, promoted Pins, "more like this" — is built around browsing and being shown things, not around a quiet, private list of links you already have and just need to find again later.

None of that makes Pinterest bad — it makes it a moodboard tool being stretched to do a bookmark manager's job.

## Where Pinterest still wins

To be fair to it: Pinterest is genuinely excellent at what it's actually for. If you're moodboarding a renovation, planning outfits, or collecting recipe photos, its massive image library, visual search, and "shop the look" features have no real equivalent in SaveBoard. SaveBoard doesn't do discovery or browsing at all — it only holds what you already found and saved. If finding new visual inspiration is the point, stay on Pinterest.
