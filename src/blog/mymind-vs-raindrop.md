# mymind vs Raindrop.io: Which Should You Use? (2026)

mymind and Raindrop.io are both well-regarded tools for saving things from the web, and they solve the problem in almost opposite ways. Raindrop gives you full manual control — collections, sub-collections, tags, filters. mymind gives you almost none of that on purpose — no folders, no tags to manage, just a search bar and AI that sorts things for you.

If you're choosing between them, the honest answer is that the choice is really about how much you want to organise things yourself.

---

## The short version

- **Choose Raindrop.io** if you want to control exactly where every bookmark lives, you're saving from a desktop browser most of the time, and you want a free plan with no bookmark cap.
- **Choose mymind** if you'd rather not organise anything — you save first and search later — and you're saving a mix of screenshots, images and links, not just URLs.
- **Neither is built primarily for saving from your phone.** If that's most of your saving — links from Instagram, YouTube, group chats — that's a real gap, and it's the one we built SaveBoard to fill (more on that at the end).

## How they're actually different

**Organisation philosophy.** Raindrop is structure-first: you create collections and nested sub-collections, and every save goes where you put it. mymind is the opposite — there's no filing system to maintain at all. You save something and mymind's AI tags and categorises it automatically; you find it later by searching or browsing, not by remembering which folder you used.

**What they're built to save.** Raindrop is bookmark-centric — it's built around URLs, with parsing, previews and (on paid plans) a full-text archive of the page. mymind is built for a wider range of things: screenshots, photos, quotes and notes alongside links, with its AI able to search inside images (recognising text and visual similarity), not just link metadata.

**Free plan.** Raindrop.io's free plan is genuinely generous — unlimited bookmarks and collections, on unlimited devices, with no bookmark cap on the free tier. mymind doesn't have an equivalent ongoing free plan: it's a paid product across its plan tiers (it names them things like "The Bookmarker" for a lighter tier up to "Mastermind" for its AI-heavy tier), so you're evaluating it as a subscription decision from day one rather than trying it indefinitely for free. We weren't able to confirm current exact prices for either plan from their own pages at the time of writing, so we're not stating dollar figures here — check their pricing pages directly for current rates.

**Platform.** Both are primarily desktop/browser tools first. Raindrop has browser extensions for every major browser, and that's arguably its strongest feature — clipping a page as you browse. mymind also leans on a browser extension and a polished web app. Both have mobile apps, but in both cases the mobile app is the secondary way to use the product, not the primary one.

## Where Raindrop.io wins

- **An unlimited free tier.** No bookmark cap, ever, on the free plan.
- **Manual control.** If you know exactly how you want things organised, Raindrop lets you build that structure and keep it.
- **Browser extensions** across every major browser, built for clipping as you browse on desktop.
- **Deep search and archiving** (full-text search and a permanent copy of the page) on its paid plan.

## Where mymind wins

- **Zero-maintenance organisation.** If tagging and filing feels like a chore, mymind's whole pitch is that you never have to do it.
- **Content beyond links.** Screenshots, moodboards, and images with AI that can find a saved image by describing what's in it, not just its title.
- **A genuinely different search experience** for visual, hard-to-tag material — the use case where AI-first sorting actually beats manual folders.
