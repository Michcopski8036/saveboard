## 1. The Android payment bug

**Commit:** [`e960433`](https://github.com/Michcopski8036/saveboard/commit/e960433bab6ec8d587679b01493cf87cc3ca5a01)
— "Android upgrade screen: stop showing the Apple IAP view" (2026-08-13). Cross-check:
`CHANGELOG.md` line for iOS 1.0.9/Android 1.0.14 states this was live since 2026-05-27.

> Android users of my app could not pay me for about 11 weeks. Not "checkout was clunky" —
> the upgrade screen literally showed them Apple's iPhone-only purchase UI, which has no
> Android backend, so prices never loaded and there was no buy button at all.
>
> One line was the cause: the upgrade screen branched on "is this a native app" instead of
> "which native app is this." iOS and Android both count as native, so Android quietly
> inherited the iOS-only payment view the day that branch was added.
>
> Fixed now: iOS keeps Apple's in-app purchase flow (App Store policy requires it), Android
> gets a plain-text pointer to the website to pay via Stripe there (Play policy doesn't allow
> in-app Stripe checkout), and web keeps its own Stripe flow. Three platforms, three different
> rules, and the bug was treating two of them as one.
>
> The unglamorous part: I only found it by reading my own code, not from a support ticket or
> a revenue graph. If you ship a native app with platform-gated payments, that branch is worth
> a second look today.

## 2. The bug only visible in the failures, not the average

**Commit:** [`198dddf`](https://github.com/Michcopski8036/saveboard/commit/198dddf5e4bff3ca493bcdd396cc2e3aa98a400f)
— "Don't spin forever when getSession() is slow" (2026-08-11).

> Found a bug in my own app by accident, while taking screenshots for something unrelated: the
> landing page rendered on 1 of 3 local reloads. Same code, same machine, different result each
> time — which is the specific kind of bug that's easy to write off as "flaky tooling" and move
> on from.
>
> It wasn't the tooling. The whole app was gated behind one auth session check with no timeout
> and no error handling. If that call was ever slow — a bad network, a slow cold start,
> whatever — every visitor sat on a bare spinner forever, including on the landing page, which
> never needed a login session in the first place.
>
> Fix was small: an 8-second timer that opens the gate regardless, plus a catch on the
> rejection path. A session that arrives late still signs the user in normally — nothing about
> "logged in" is faked, only "stuck" gets a ceiling. After the fix: 5 of 5 local reloads
> render.
>
> The lesson I keep re-learning: "works on my machine, most of the time" is exactly the shape
> a load-bearing bug likes to hide in.

## 3. A sidebar that forgets what you told it, every single reload

**Commit:** [`d794087`](https://github.com/Michcopski8036/saveboard/commit/d794087b2c1920b79994f9cc9b244d14777e6f49)
— "Let users pin or collapse the sidebar" (2026-08-10).

> Small feature, deceptively fiddly build: letting people collapse the sidebar and have it
> *stay* collapsed.
>
> Before this, the sidebar's width was decided purely by your browser's viewport — locked open
> at one size above a breakpoint, locked to a narrow icon rail below it, with no way to choose
> either way for yourself. Even the one place you could nudge it open was a temporary overlay
> that snapped shut the moment you clicked into a different board.
>
> The fix meant splitting one boolean that had been secretly doing two jobs (mobile drawer +
> desktop width) into two separate pieces of state, one of which now persists to
> localStorage per device. The fiddly part was CSS: Tailwind can't see a width value decided at
> runtime, so the collapsed/expanded width now flows through a CSS custom property on the app
> root instead of a Tailwind class. And collapsing the sidebar couldn't be allowed to break
> drag-and-drop — dropping a saved link onto a board still has to work when boards are just
> icons in a thin rail, not full rows with names.
>
> Verified by hand at three different widths (1800 / 1317 / 584px): toggle, reload, switch
> boards, resize the window. All of it held.
